# Actors Finding — Collective and Institutional Agency

**Status:** current topic finding  
**Scope:** Actors-local distinctions; not a theory that all organizations are agents

## Current finding

Plurality alone does not create a collective Actor, but sufficiently organized collectives can support group-level attitudes, decisions or actions that are not accurately represented as a simple sum or majority of member-level states.

The foundation must preserve both collective-level and member-level attribution without assuming either universal reducibility or universal group personhood.

## Distinctions to preserve

### 1. Collection ≠ organized group agent

Christian List distinguishes aggregate, common and corporate attitudes. Aggregate and common attitudes can be attributed to a collection without treating it as a group agent; corporate attitudes depend on organizational structure and decision procedures and carry the stronger group-agency commitment.

### 2. Group-level attitude/action ≠ majority member attitude

A corporate attitude may be produced by an organizational decision procedure and diverge from the attitudes of most individual members. `The organization decided X` therefore cannot safely be translated into `most members wanted X`.

### 3. Organizational action can be realized through individuals without being identical to one individual's action

Collectives generally act through members, representatives, offices or systems. That implementation fact does not by itself settle the level at which a decision or action is institutionally attributed.

### 4. Group responsibility ≠ distributed individual responsibility

Collective responsibility literature allows genuinely collective responsibility while also allowing individual members to bear separate responsibility. The two levels need not be mutually exclusive or numerically identical.

### 5. Decision procedure and organizational structure are semantically important

If a group-level action or attitude depends on voting, quorum, authorization chain, office structure, deliberation or another procedure, the procedure is not incidental metadata. It can determine whether a corporate attribution is warranted and whose participation matters.

## Decision-critical implication

The collective-reduction assumption is rejected, but so is the opposite assumption that every named group is automatically a first-class agent.

A later synthesis must be able to ask:

- what collective or organization is being discussed;
- what organizational/decision structure licenses the group-level attribution;
- which members, offices or representatives participated and how;
- whether the statement is aggregate, common or corporate in character;
- whether group-level and individual-level responsibility coexist.

The research does not prescribe List's vocabulary as the SOUL model; it uses the distinction to falsify naive collapse.

## Evidence used

### Christian List — `Three Kinds of Collective Attitudes`

Inspected view:
- author's LSE-hosted PDF, including abstract, distinction among aggregate/common/corporate attitudes, and later discussion of organizational generation and divergence from majority member attitudes.
- page-level screenshots were inspected for the relevant sections.

Source:
- https://personal.lse.ac.uk/list/PDF-files/CollectiveAttitudes.pdf

Supports:
- not every collective attitude implies group agency;
- organized corporate attitudes can be action-guiding and differ from member-majority attitudes;
- organizational structure/decision procedures matter.

Does not establish:
- that every organization is a moral or legal person;
- a universal threshold for group agency.

Source:

Supports:
- collective moral responsibility is a distinct and contested problem;
- collective responsibility does not require eliminating separate member responsibility.

## Residual uncertainty

- The minimum organizational conditions for treating a collective as an agent vary by domain and remain contested.
- Legal personality, moral agency, operational group agency and institutional decision attribution must not be assumed coextensive.
- Succession, merger, reorganization and changing membership create continuity questions that belong to later modelling or domain-specific research unless they become decision-critical in synthesis.
