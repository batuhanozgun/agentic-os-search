### Child 1 — Constituent Composition / Membership

Primary question:

> **Which materially relevant constituents actually participate in or make up the selected whole/scope, including materially relevant part–whole and nested membership?**

Protects errors such as:
- missing / extra / wrong constituent;
- wrong membership or participation boundary;
- wrong part–whole decomposition;
- wrong nesting/aggregation;
- treating an inventory/list/model as proof of realized membership.

### Child 2 — Relational Organization / Topology

Primary question:

> **How are the relevant constituents actually structurally related, connected, arranged, contained or interfaced at the selected scope/time?**

Protects errors such as:
- right constituents, wrong connections/dependencies;
- wrong topology/routing/adjacency;
- wrong spatial arrangement or containment relation;
- wrong structural interface/boundary relation;
- design/topology model mistaken for realized organization.

## Why the parent label changes

`Composition` is too narrow as a co-primary synonym for the whole node: a constituent set/part hierarchy can be correct while realized connections, topology or arrangement are wrong. `Structure` is the broader surviving node; composition is one of its immediate problem regions.

## Why two children now survive

At the Domain-State parent level, constituents and relations were economically bundled because both belong to Structure. Deeper Structure-specific research changes the resolution:
- mereological/assembly questions can fail independently of connection/topology questions;
- network/non-hierarchical systems require relational organization even when the constituent set is known;
- cross-domain cases repeatedly distinguish participation/composition from links/interactions/placement;
- recovery questions differ enough to satisfy the child-promotion test.

This is not a return to graph primitives. The split is semantic:
- `Composition / Membership` asks **what participates / what is part of what**;
- `Relational Organization / Topology` asks **how those relevant constituents are structurally organized**.
